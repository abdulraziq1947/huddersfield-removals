## Essential packing materials for fragile items

Before you start, gather the right supplies. Cheap or reused boxes that have lost rigidity are a common cause of breakages.

### What you need

- **Sturdy double-walled boxes** in small and medium sizes, heavy fragile items belong in smaller boxes
- **Bubble wrap** for glasses, ornaments and electronics
- **Packing paper** (unprinted newsprint) for wrapping and filling gaps
- **Foam pouches** for plates and bowls
- **Packing tape**: apply generously along box seams
- **Marker pens** for labelling
- **Corner protectors and cardboard sheets** for mirrors, artwork and TV screens
- **Stretch wrap** to secure drawers and loose parts on furniture

## How to pack glassware and china

### Glasses and stemware

Wrap each glass individually in packing paper, tucking the paper into the bowl of the glass. For stemware, wrap the stem separately and add extra padding around it. Place glasses upright in a divided box if available, or nest them with paper between each piece. Never pack glasses on their side in a loose box.

### Plates and bowls

Wrap each plate in packing paper. Stack three to four wrapped plates together and wrap the bundle again. **Pack plates vertically** (on their edge), not flat, this distributes weight and reduces pressure on the bottom plate during transport. Fill gaps with scrunched paper so the stack cannot move.

### Mugs and teapots

Stuff mugs with paper and wrap the outside. Teapots and lids should be wrapped separately, then taped together to prevent loss. Place in a box with plenty of cushioning above and below.

## How to pack electronics safely

Electronics are fragile in a different way, they are sensitive to shock, static and moisture.

### TVs and monitors

If you kept the original box, use it. Otherwise, wrap the screen in bubble wrap (bubbles facing out), add a rigid cardboard face protector, and place in a box sized to fit with minimal movement. Label "FRAGILE, SCREEN" and keep upright during loading and unloading.

### Computers and games consoles

Back up data before the move. Remove cables, label them, and bag them with the device. Wrap the unit in anti-static bubble wrap and pack in a box with foam or paper cushioning on all sides. Do not pack heavy items on top.

### Small appliances

Remove loose parts, blender jugs, kettle lids, microwave turntables. Wrap cords neatly and secure with tape or twist ties. Pack each appliance in a box with padding, or wrap in bubble wrap if placing in a larger carton.

## How to pack mirrors, artwork and frames

Never pack mirrors or framed pictures flat in a stack, the weight of other boxes can crack glass.

Wrap the piece in bubble wrap, add corner protectors, and sandwich between two rigid cardboard sheets taped together. Mark clearly which side is glass. Stand artwork vertically in the van, secured between soft furniture or dedicated picture boxes.

## How to pack lamps and light fittings

Remove shades and pack them in separate boxes with tissue paper, never crush a shade under other items. Wrap the base in bubble wrap. Pack shades box-to-box without stacking heavy cartons on top. Note any fittings you disassemble for reassembly at your new home in Huddersfield or elsewhere in West Yorkshire.

## Labelling and loading fragile boxes

Every fragile box should be marked on **all sides** with:

- "FRAGILE"
- "THIS WAY UP" arrows where orientation matters
- Destination room (e.g. Kitchen, Lounge)

During loading and unloading, fragile cartons should be placed on top of heavier boxes, secured with straps, and kept away from items that could puncture or crush them. ## Fragile packing checklist

- Gather packing materials before you start
- Wrap every item individually, no bare glass or ceramic touching another piece
- Use small boxes for heavy fragile items
- Pack plates vertically; fill all gaps with paper
- Label every box clearly on multiple sides
- Keep a "do not pack" pile for items travelling with you in the car
- Photograph valuable items before packing for inventory purposes
- Confirm your removal quote covers the volume of fragile goods
