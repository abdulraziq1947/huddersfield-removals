Books are deceptive. A single paperback weighs nothing; a large box full of them weighs more than most people can lift safely. Every removals crew has a story about a "book box" packed in a wardrobe carton. Here's how to do it properly.

## Use small boxes, always

The golden rule of book packing: **small boxes only**. A small double-wall box filled with books lands around 15–20kg, heavy but liftable. The same books in a large box would top 40kg, break the box or the back lifting it.

If you're using our [packing materials](/packing-materials/), the "small" in the range is sized for exactly this job.

## Pack by position

- **Flat, in stacks**: best for most books. Spines alternate direction to keep stacks level.
- **Spine down**: acceptable for filling gaps. Never spine *up*: the pages splay and the binding takes the load.
- **Never pack books at odd angles** to squeeze more in. Warped covers are the result.

## Protect the ones that matter

For signed copies, first editions or sentimental volumes, wrap each book in packing paper (not newspaper, the ink transfers) before boxing. Line the box base with crumpled paper. If a book is genuinely valuable, our terms ask you to flag high-value items in advance, tell us and we'll take particular care, or transport it yourself for total peace of mind.

## Fill the gaps, then label honestly

Half-empty boxes crush when stacked. Fill voids with crumpled paper, then label with the room *and* the contents, "BOOKS, front bedroom: HEAVY" tells the crew exactly what they're lifting and where it's going.

## The declutter question

Books are heavy, and weight is effort. Before packing three shelves you haven't touched since the last move, consider a pass through: Huddersfield's charity shops take good-condition books gladly. What's left is a collection, not a burden.
