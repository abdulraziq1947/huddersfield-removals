Children experience a house move completely differently from adults. Adults see logistics and mortgages; children see their whole known world being put into boxes. A little deliberate handling turns the move from something that happens *to* them into something they're part of.

## Before the move: tell them early and honestly

Children cope better with change they can see coming. Once dates are real, explain the move in concrete terms: where, when, what stays the same. Visit the new area if you can, the park, the school gates, the walk to the shops. Familiarity in advance shrinks the unknown.

**Under-fives** mostly need reassurance that people, pets and favourite things are all coming. A picture book about moving does more than a conversation.

**Primary age** children want agency. Let them pack a box of their own things, choose their new room's colour, and carry their own backpack of treasures on the day.

**Teenagers** are losing a social world, which is genuinely hard. Acknowledge it rather than selling the upsides. Good wifi on day one and transport links back to old friends matter more than the new bedroom.

## On moving day

- Pack each child a **going-first bag**: comfort object, pyjamas, toothbrush, snacks, tablet and charger.
- For toddlers, moving day with grandparents or friends is often kinder than a day of doors, boxes and crews, and safer too.
- If children are present, make them greeters at the new house: their job is to tell the crew which room is theirs. (Our crews play along gladly.)
- Set up children's bedrooms **first**: before the kitchen, before the sofa. A familiar duvet in a strange house is the single best settling tool there is.

## The weeks after

Keep bedtimes and mealtimes on their usual schedule from night one, routine is a child's evidence that the world still works. Explore the new neighbourhood in small doses, and get school starts organised quickly; children settle when their weekdays have shape again.

Expect a wobble two or three weeks in, after the novelty fades. It's normal, and it passes.
