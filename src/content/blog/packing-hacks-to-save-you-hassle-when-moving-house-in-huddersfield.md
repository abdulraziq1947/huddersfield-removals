We see the inside of hundreds of moves a year, which means we see which packing "hacks" actually work and which ones create carnage in the back of a van. Here's the honest list.

## The ones that genuinely work

**Clothes stay on hangers.** Group hanging clothes and either use wardrobe cartons (best) or bundle them inside a bin bag with the hangers poking out (budget version). Fold-and-box is wasted effort you'll re-iron later.

**A "first night" box per person.** Kettle, mugs, tea, phone chargers, toiletries, a towel, one change of clothes, medication. Label it loudly and load it last so it comes off first.

**Photograph the cable spaghetti.** One photo of the back of the TV saves half an hour of guesswork at the other end. Tape remote controls to the device they belong to.

**Plates on their edge.** Wrapped plates packed vertically, like records, survive; stacked flat, the bottom plate carries the whole tower's weight over every pothole.

**Colour-code by room.** A roll of coloured tape per room, matching tape on the door frames at the new house. The crew stops asking and starts flowing.

**Use luggage for weight.** Wheeled suitcases are made for heavy loads, fill them with books and let the wheels work.

## The ones that backfire

**Giant boxes "to save boxes".** A large box of kitchenware is a broken box. Heavy items go in small boxes, no exceptions.

**Bin bags for everything.** Fine for duvets and soft toys; terrible for anything with corners. Bags can't stack, so they cost van space and get crushed.

**Leaving drawers full.** Furniture with loaded drawers is heavier, the drawers slide open mid-carry, and chipboard racks under the strain. Empty them.

**Packing an "everything" box at the end.** That mystery box of remote controls, screws, keys and chargers will haunt you. Slow down for the last 10%, it's where lost things come from.
