After enough moves, you learn that damage isn't random. The same five categories account for most breakage claims across the industry, and each one has a known fix. Here they are, in order of frequency.

## 1. Glassware and crockery

**Why it breaks:** packed flat, packed loose, or packed in oversized boxes where the weight of the stack crushes the bottom layer.

**The fix:** small double-wall boxes, every piece wrapped, plates on their edges like records, voids filled with paper so nothing shifts. And a hard truth from our terms: fragile items are covered for breakage when *we* pack them, self-packed china travels at your risk. If it matters, let the [packing team](/packing-services/) do that one box.

## 2. Televisions and monitors

**Why it breaks:** screens are strong against the world in front of them and fragile against pressure from behind or twisting in transit.

**The fix:** original box if you kept it; otherwise bubble wrap, a screen-sized flat carton, and vertical transport, never laid flat under other items. Photograph the screen working before the move; it settles any dispute instantly.

## 3. Mattresses

**Why it gets ruined rather than broken:** dragged across floors, folded into cars, or loaded unprotected against a dusty van wall. Grime and tears, not snaps.

**The fix:** a polythene mattress cover (a few pounds, [we sell them](/packing-materials/)) and upright transport strapped against a padded surface. Never fold a pocket-sprung mattress, the springs don't forgive it.

## 4. Flat-pack furniture

**Why it breaks:** chipboard is engineered to be assembled once and stay still. Carrying a built wardrobe down stairs applies forces it was never designed for; racking splits the joints.

**The fix:** dismantle before transport, move as flat panels, rebuild at the other end. It's exactly why [dismantling and reassembly](/furniture-dismantling-and-reassembly/) exists as a service, and per our terms, reassembled flat-pack can never be guaranteed factory-perfect, which is one more argument for letting careful hands do it.

## 5. White goods

**Why they break:** washing machines travel without their transit bolts and destroy their drum bearings; fridges get tipped and their compressor oil migrates.

**The fix:** refit transit bolts (taped to the back is where they usually live), keep fridges upright and let them stand before switch-on, and make sure everything is disconnected, defrosted and dry before the crew arrives.
