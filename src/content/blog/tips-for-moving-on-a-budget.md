A tight budget doesn't mean a bad move, but it does mean choosing your savings carefully. Some cuts are free; some are booby-trapped. Here's how to tell them apart.

## Savings that are genuinely free

**Own less before you move.** Volume is the engine of moving costs. A ruthless declutter, sell, donate, give away, can drop a move a whole van load. Selling unwanted furniture can even put the budget in credit.

**Be flexible on dates.** Midweek beats Friday; mid-month beats month-end; winter beats summer. If your dates can bend, your price usually will too.

**Do the prep yourself.** Self-packing, dismantling your own flat-pack, emptying drawers, defrosting the freezer, sorting parking, every hour of prep is crew time you're not paying for.

**Borrow the muscle-free jobs.** Friends can pack books and clean the oven. Keep the piano and the wardrobe for professionals.

## Savings that backfire

**The cheapest unvetted van.** No insurance figures, no written quote, no reviews, when it goes wrong, it costs multiples of what it saved. At minimum, confirm goods-in-transit and public liability cover in writing (ours: £50k per load and £1m, published in our terms).

**Free supermarket boxes for everything.** Fine for towels; a disaster for crockery. Single-wall banana boxes collapse when stacked in a van. Spend a little on double-wall boxes for the heavy and fragile, [we deliver them locally](/packing-materials/).

**Moving big items yourself "to save a trip".** A sofa down a Huddersfield terrace staircase is how backs and banisters get broken. The A&E maths never works out.

**Skipping the survey.** Guessing your own volume leads to a van that's too small, which means a second trip, which means the cheap quote just doubled.

## The budget-move formula

Declutter hard, pack yourself with proper boxes, prep everything before the crew arrives, move midweek, and spend what's left of the budget on an insured professional crew for the loading, driving and unloading. That split gets you 80% of the savings with none of the horror stories.
