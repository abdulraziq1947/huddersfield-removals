The honest answer: it depends on four things, how much you own, how far you're going, how easy both properties are to work in, and whether you want packing done for you. Here's how each one moves the number, so you can read any quote (ours included) with confidence.

## The four price drivers

**Volume.** Bedrooms are the industry shorthand for van loads. A one-bed flat is typically a single 3.5-tonne Luton load; a three-bed family house is usually two loads or a carefully packed one. Every box you declutter before moving day is money saved.

**Distance.** A move within Huddersfield, say Marsh to Almondbury, keeps drive time to minutes, so you're mostly paying for loading and unloading. Long-distance moves to London or Scotland add fuel, motorway hours and sometimes an overnight element.

**Access.** This is the factor people forget. A fourth-floor flat with no lift, a steep Colne Valley terrace with no parking, or a 40-metre carry from the nearest legal stopping point all add crew time. It's why a proper company asks about access before quoting, and why suspiciously cheap quotes often grow on moving day.

**Packing.** A full professional packing service adds cost but saves you several days of work, and it changes your insurance position: with our service, fragile items are covered for breakage only when our team packed them.

## Ways the price structure can catch you out

- **Hourly rates with no estimate of hours.** Fine for small jobs, risky for full houses. Ask what the total is likely to be, in writing.
- **Quotes without a survey.** Nobody can price a four-bed house accurately from a phone call. A quick in-person or video survey protects both sides.
- **Missing insurance detail.** Ask for goods-in-transit and public liability figures. Ours are £50,000 per vehicle load and £1 million respectively, stated in our terms.
- **Deposit and cancellation terms.** Read them before booking, not after. Ours are published in full in our terms and conditions.
