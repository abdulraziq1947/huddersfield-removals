Later-life moves are different. They're often downsizes from a long-held family home, which means decades of belongings and decades of memories in the same boxes. The logistics are the easy part, done well, the whole process should respect the pace and the person.

## Start earlier than feels necessary

A family home accumulates. Give the sorting phase weeks, not days, and treat it as several short sessions rather than one exhausting weekend. One room at a time; one decision at a time.

A method that works: four zones, **coming along, family and friends, charity, disposal**. Photographing sentimental items that can't make the move preserves the memory without the volume.

## Downsizing without the guilt

The hardest part is rarely the furniture, it's the significance attached to it. Some things that help:

- Offer meaningful pieces to family *early*, so there's time for collection before moving week.
- Keep a "first night" box of genuine essentials: medication, glasses, kettle, favourite mug, phone charger, a change of clothes.
- Accept that some things will go to charity, and that being used again is a good ending for them.

## Settling into the new place

Unpack the sitting room and bedroom first, the rooms where comfort lives. Lamps, photos and familiar textiles on day one make an enormous psychological difference. The rest of the boxes can wait.
