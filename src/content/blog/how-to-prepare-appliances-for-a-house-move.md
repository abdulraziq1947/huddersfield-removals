Appliances are the items most often not-ready when a crew arrives, and under our terms (like every remover's), they need to be **disconnected, empty, clean and dry** before they travel. Here's exactly what that means, appliance by appliance.

## Washing machine

The big one: **refit the transit bolts.** They lock the drum for transport; without them, the drum bounces on its suspension and the bearings pay for it. They're usually taped to the back panel or in the manual bag, if lost, appliance spares shops stock generic sets.

Beyond that: run an empty hot wash a few days out, disconnect, drain both hoses (there's always a litre hiding), and leave the door ajar to dry.

## Fridge and freezer

Empty, defrost 24–48 hours ahead, wipe dry, tape the doors for transport. Keep it upright in the van, and let it stand at the new house before switching on, four hours minimum, longer if it travelled tilted. Full detail in our [fridge-moving guide](/blog/easy-tips-for-moving-your-fridge/).

## Dishwasher

Run empty, disconnect, and drain thoroughly, dishwashers hold more residual water than anything else in the kitchen. Tape the racks so they don't launch themselves in transit.

## Cookers and hobs

**Gas appliances must be disconnected by a Gas Safe registered engineer**: that's a legal safety matter, not our crew's job or yours. Book it before moving week. Electric cookers on a cooker circuit similarly need an electrician to disconnect safely. Freestanding plug-in ovens just need to be clean and cool.

## Tumble dryer

Empty the water tank on condenser and heat-pump models, clean the lint filter, and disconnect any vent hose. Heat-pump dryers, like fridges, prefer staying upright and appreciate a settling period before first use.

## Small appliances

Kettles and toasters emptied of water and crumbs, boxed with their cables. Microwaves: take the glass plate out and wrap it separately, it's the part that always breaks.
