Fridges are one of the most commonly damaged items in DIY moves, not because they're fragile, but because they punish shortcuts. Here's the preparation routine our crews rely on.

## Two days before: empty and defrost

A fridge-freezer needs to be **empty, defrosted, clean and dry** before it travels, it's a standard condition in our terms, and for good reason. A freezer defrosting in the back of a van leaks water over everything beneath it.

- Start running down food a week ahead.
- Switch off 24–48 hours before the move; prop doors open with towels laid beneath.
- Once defrosted, wipe the interior dry and leave doors open to air.

## Moving day: keep it upright

Compressors sit in a bath of oil. Tip the unit on its side and that oil can run into the refrigerant lines, the classic cause of a fridge that never cools properly again.

- Move it upright on a dolly or sack truck wherever possible.
- If it must tilt (stairs, low doorways), keep the angle shallow and brief.
- Tape doors shut or strap them, a swinging fridge door on a staircase is dangerous.
- Protect the exterior with blankets; the coils at the back are more delicate than the sides.

## At the new house: wait before switching on

The rule of thumb: **let it stand upright for at least four hours** before plugging in, 24 hours if it travelled on its side at any point. This lets compressor oil settle back where it belongs. Then run it empty for a few hours to reach temperature before restocking.
