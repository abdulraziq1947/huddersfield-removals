Packing order matters more than packing speed. Start in the wrong place and you'll live for weeks in a house where nothing works; start in the right place and daily life barely notices until moving week. Here's the sequence our crews recommend.

## Three to four weeks out: the invisible stuff

Start with everything you won't miss:

- **Loft, garage, shed and under-stairs**: the storage zones. (Remember: per our terms, loft collections on moving day need safe access agreed in advance, so getting the loft done yourself early simplifies everything.)
- **Out-of-season everything**: winter coats in June, garden furniture cushions in January.
- **Books, DVDs, ornaments and pictures**: decorative, not functional. A home without wall art still works.
- **The formal dining set** you use twice a year.

This phase is also the declutter phase: every storage zone is half things you forgot you owned.

## Two weeks out: the sometimes stuff

- Spare bedding and towels (keep one set per person out).
- Most of the wardrobe, leave a two-week capsule.
- Kitchen gadgets beyond the daily core: the mixer, the fondue set, the good china.
- Toys on rotation (with small children, pack from the bottom of the toy pile, they won't notice).

Label every box with **room + contents + a HEAVY flag** where it applies. Future-you unpacking is a stranger who needs instructions.

## Moving week: the real life

- Remaining clothes, daily kitchen kit down to one pan and a plate per person.
- Disconnect and defrost the fridge-freezer 24–48 hours out; washing machine disconnected, drained and transit-bolted.
- Dismantle beds and wardrobes if you're doing it yourself, or leave them for [our crew](/furniture-dismantling-and-reassembly/).

## The last morning: the survival kit

One box (or bag) per person, travelling in your car, not the van: kettle, mugs, tea, phone chargers, medication, toiletries, loo roll, snacks, keys, documents. Plus a toolbox and cleaning basics for both ends.

## The one-line rule

**Pack from least-used to most-used, and label like you'll never see the packer again.** Do that, and unpacking becomes a sequence instead of an excavation.
