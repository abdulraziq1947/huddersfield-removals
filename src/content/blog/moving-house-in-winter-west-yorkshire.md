Nobody chooses January for the romance of it, winter moves happen because completions and tenancies don't check the forecast. The good news: winter moving in West Yorkshire is a solved problem, and it even has advantages. Here's what changes when the temperature drops.

## The upsides nobody mentions

- **Availability.** Winter is the quiet season, so you get first pick of dates and crews, including the popular Saturdays that book out weeks ahead in summer.
- **Cool-weather advantages.** No van-oven melting your candles and chocolate, and crews work faster in 5° than 25°.

## The genuine challenges

**Daylight.** Mid-December gives you roughly 8am to 4pm of useful light. The answer is an early start, it's why we open at 7am year-round. A move loaded by 10am is unpacking in daylight.

**Huddersfield's hills.** Our steep terraced streets: Golcar, Longwood, Newsome, the Colne Valley villages, demand respect in ice. Crews plan the van position for the gradient, salt the ramp path if needed, and slow down rather than soldier on. Build slack into the day's schedule and nobody has to rush on wet stone steps.

**Rain on everything.** Yorkshire winter is wetter than it is snowy. Mattresses go in polythene covers, sofas in stretch wrap, and the van gets loaded with wet-weather sequencing, upholstery deepest, weather-tolerant items nearest the door.

## Protecting both houses

- Lay a runner of flattened cardboard or dust sheets on the main carry route, wet boots plus new carpet is the classic winter claim.
- Keep one room's heating on at each end: a warm base room for kettle, paperwork and morale.
- Have towels by the door for hands, boxes and the dog.

## Your winter-move checklist

- Book heating oil/gas and utility switchovers so the new house is warm on night one.
- Keep de-icer, a torch and warm layers in the car, not the van.
- Confirm parking for the van early, winter roadside space disappears under weather and works.
- Check the new home's stopcock and fuse board location *in daylight*.
